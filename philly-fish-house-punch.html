Philly Fish House Punch

Philadelphia Fish House Punch, the 1732 original of the Schuylkill Fishing Company ("State in Schuylkill"). Done properly: a real oleo-saccharum, true peach brandy (not peach schnapps or liqueur), and cold black tea for the dilution. Party batch: makes about 5.7 liters — fifty 4-oz servings. Since there's no ice block diluting the bowl, serve it well chilled and keep the pours small. Start the oleo-saccharum at least 3 hours ahead. You'll need a 2-gallon punch bowl or dispenser, or split across two pitchers.

Ingredients

Oleo-saccharum

  • 16 lemons, peels only (use a Y-peeler, avoid the white pith; reserve the lemons for juicing)
  • 680 grams demerara or raw sugar (3 1/2 cups)

Punch

  • 950 ml fresh lemon juice (4 cups, from the peeled lemons plus ~6 more)
  • 1500 ml Jamaican rum (two bottles; something with funk — Smith & Cross, Appleton Estate, or a blend)
  • 750 ml cognac (one bottle, VS or VSOP)
  • 300 ml true peach brandy (most of a 375ml bottle — a barrel-aged peach eau-de-vie, not peach schnapps or crème de pêche)
  • 2 liters strong black tea, brewed and fully chilled (8 cups water + 8 bags or 16 grams loose black tea)
  • 1 whole nutmeg, for grating (optional)
  • 3 lemons, sliced into thin wheels, for the bowl

Directions

  1. 15 minutesthen 3+ hoursPeel the 16 lemons with a Y-peeler in wide strips, taking as little pith as possible. In a large bowl, muddle the peels firmly with the 680g sugar until the sugar looks wet and smells strongly of lemon. Cover and let stand at room temperature for at least 3 hours (up to overnight), muddling again once or twice — the sugar pulls the oils from the peels into a fragrant syrup. This oleo-saccharum is the backbone of the punch; don't skip or shortcut it.
  2. 10 minutesthen chillBrew the tea: steep 8 bags or 16g black tea in 8 cups just-boiled water for 4 minutes — no longer, or the tannin turns harsh. Remove the tea, cool to room temperature, then refrigerate until fully chilled.
  3. 20 minutesJuice the peeled lemons plus extras to get 950 ml lemon juice, and strain out pulp and seeds. Refrigerate.
  4. 5 minutesWhen the oleo-saccharum is ready, pour the 950 ml lemon juice over the peels and sugar, and stir until every trace of sugar is dissolved. Strain out and discard the peels.
  5. 5 minutesIn the punch bowl or dispenser, combine the lemon-oleo mixture with the 1500 ml rum, 750 ml cognac, and 300 ml peach brandy. Stir, then add the 2 liters chilled tea. Taste — it should be strong but balanced; add a splash more tea if it bites.
  6. 0 minutesthen 2+ hoursRefrigerate the whole vessel (or the mixture split across pitchers) until party time — with no ice in the bowl, serving it thoroughly chilled is what matters. The punch improves as it sits and the flavors marry; it can be built up to a day ahead.
  7. 5 minutesJust before guests arrive, float the 3 lemons' worth of wheels on top and grate a little nutmeg over the surface if you like. Serve in small cups (3–4 oz pours) — this is a famously deceptive punch, roughly a full cocktail's worth of spirit per small cup; small pours are traditional and wise.

Substitutions: if true peach brandy is unfindable, the least-bad fallback is 150 ml peach liqueur + 150 ml extra cognac — but seek out the real thing (Kuchan, Peach Street Distillers, or similar aged peach eau-de-vie). Plain cold water is historically correct in place of tea, but the tea's light tannin adds welcome structure. If serving over a long evening in a warm room, you can add a modest ice block to the bowl — if you do, hold back ~500ml of the tea to keep the balance as it melts.

Full example schedule

  1. 12:00 PM: Peel lemons, muddle with sugar, rest 3+ hours (oleo-saccharum)
  2. 12:20 PM: Brew tea, cool, refrigerate
  3. 2:00 PM: Juice lemons, strain, refrigerate
  4. 3:00 PM: Dissolve oleo-saccharum in lemon juice, strain out peels
  5. 3:10 PM: Combine with rum, cognac, peach brandy, and tea; taste
  6. 3:15 PM: Refrigerate punch until serving
  7. 5:30 PM: Add lemon wheels and nutmeg; serve in small cups
