Trofie with Pistachio, Artichokes, and Orange Zest

From "The Pasta Book" by Marc Vetri

Serves: 4-6Estimated Total Time: ~1 hour 20 minutesMakes: ~480g (about 1 pound); 4-6 servingsRest Time: 30 minutes

Pistachio Pesto

Ingredient Amount
Shelled, unsalted raw pistachios 1 cup (130g)
Extra-virgin olive oil ⅔ cup
Kosher salt to taste

Artichokes

Ingredient Amount
Lemon juice from ½ lemon
Baby artichokes 6

Trofie

Ingredient Amount
Kosher salt for pasta water
Fresh Trofie 1 pound (from Hand-Rolled Dough below)
Extra-virgin olive oil 2 tablespoons
Freshly ground black pepper to taste
Parmesan cheese, finely grated ¼ cup, plus more for garnish
Orange zest, finely grated or julienned from 1 orange

Method

Make the Pistachio Pesto

In a blender, grind the pistachios and oil until the nuts are very finely chopped but not completely pureed, 1-2 minutes, adding a little water if necessary to get everything to blend. Taste and season with salt (about ¼ teaspoon). You should have about 1½ cups pesto. Measure out 1 cup for the pasta; save the rest for sandwiches or freeze for up to 1 month.

Prep the Artichokes

In a medium bowl, combine the lemon juice and 1½ cups cold water. Working with one artichoke at a time, snap off and discard all the fibrous outer leaves. Using a paring knife, peel until you are left with only the very tender, pale-yellow leaves in a bullet shape about 1 inch by ½ inch in size. Slice lengthwise into wedges about ¼ inch thick and drop into the lemon water. Repeat with remaining artichokes.

Cook the Trofie

Bring a large pot of water to a boil and add salt until it tastes like well-seasoned broth. Drop in the trofie, stir, and cover to quickly return to a boil. Uncover or partially cover and boil, stirring occasionally, until tender but still a little chewy, 2-3 minutes.

Meanwhile, in a deep 12-inch sauté pan, heat the olive oil over medium heat. Drain the artichokes, add to the pan, and cook, stirring once or twice, until mostly tender but still with a little bite, 3-5 minutes.

When the pasta is ready, use a spider strainer to transfer it to the pan with artichokes. Add about 1 cup pistachio pesto and 1 cup hot pasta water. Shake and swirl the pan until the sauce thickens and begins to hug the pasta, 1-2 minutes. Season with salt and pepper to taste.

Remove from heat, add the Parmesan, and keep tossing until the sauce thickens further and the pasta and sauce marry, leaving little to no sauce in the pan.

Serve

Dish out onto warmed plates, building into a mound. Garnish with orange zest and additional Parmesan if desired.

Hand-Rolled Dough

Makes: ~480g (about 1 pound); 4-6 servings Rest Time: 30 minutes

Ingredients

Ingredient Amount
Semolina flour (such as Caputo) 330g (2 cups)
Water 150g (scant ⅔ cup)

Method

By Hand

Pile the flour into a mountain on your countertop, make a well in the center, and add the water. Mix the flour in from the edges until combined into a cohesive mass. Knead until stiff yet rollable like Play-Doh, about 5 minutes.

By Stand Mixer

Pour the semolina into the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment. Turn to low speed and gradually add the water. Mix on low for about 5 minutes. The mixture will start crumbly, then clumps will get bigger as the flour hydrates. The dough should start clumping around the paddle and the mixer should struggle as it stiffens. If still in small crumbs, drizzle in more water, 1 tablespoon at a time.

Remove the paddle and squeeze the dough together in the bowl into a single mass. Transfer to work surface and knead briefly with hands to form a smoother ball.

Rest

Cover and set aside for 30 minutes so the gluten can relax. The dough should have an even pale-yellow color throughout and feel stiff yet rollable, like stiff Play-Doh. Use immediately or wrap in plastic and rest at room temperature for up to 6 hours.

Trofie Shape

  1. Pinch off about one-quarter of the dough; keep the rest covered with a damp towel
  2. Moisten your work surface with a damp towel for stiction
  3. Pinch off a nickel-size piece of dough and place on the work surface
  4. Roll into a slug shape with your palm
  5. Hover the meaty outside part of your palm over it, bottom of palm near top of dough
  6. Using heavy pressure, smoosh down and roll your palm over the dough toward your body diagonally, starting with bottom of palm and ending with top
  7. A corkscrew shape should twist out—each should be 1-2 inches long and a little less than ¼ inch diameter
  8. Flick onto a lightly semolina-floured area or sheet pan
  9. Repeat with remaining dough

Storage: Cover loosely and use within 1 hour, or refrigerate up to 1 day, or freeze in a single layer then transfer to airtight container for up to 1 month. Cook frozen pasta directly from freezer.
