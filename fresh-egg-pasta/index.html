Fresh Egg Pasta

Main

Serious Eats' workhorse egg dough — two whole eggs plus four extra yolks per 9 ounces of all-purpose flour, which lands between an all-yolk dough (rich but too stiff to roll thin) and a wetter one that sticks to the roller. Springy enough for fettuccine, pliable enough for ravioli. Makes about 1 pound.

Serves: 4-6Estimated Total Time: ~1 hour 35 minutes

Ingredients

  • 255 grams all-purpose flour (9 oz; about 2 cups), plus more for dusting
  • 2 whole large eggs (110g)
  • 4 yolks from large eggs (70g)
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more for the pasta water

Special equipment: pasta roller, bench scraper, rolling pin.

Directions

  1. 10 minutesPour the 255g flour onto a clean work surface in a mound and make a well in the center at least 4 inches wide. Add the 2 whole eggs, 4 yolks, and 1 tsp kosher salt to the well and beat thoroughly with a fork, then gradually pull flour in from the edges until a wet, sticky dough forms.
  2. 5 minutesScrape the dough off the fork and your fingers with a bench scraper. Fold the remaining flour in with the scraper, rotating the dough about 45° each time, until it feels firm and dry and holds together as a craggy ball.
  3. 10 minutesKnead: press the heel of your hand forward and down into the ball, rotate 45°, repeat. Keep going until the dough is smooth and elastic, like a firm ball of Play-Doh. If it's tacky, add flour 1 tsp at a time; if it's genuinely crumbly, mist with water from a spray bottle rather than pouring.
  4. 30 minutesWrap tightly in plastic and rest on the counter 30 minutes. Meanwhile, dust a parchment-lined tray with flour.
  5. 25 minutesCut the dough into quarters, rewrap three, and flatten one with a rolling pin into an oblong about ½ inch thick. Pass it through the pasta roller on its widest setting 3 times. Fold both ends in to meet at the center, fold in half at that seam, flatten back to ½ inch, and pass through the widest setting 3 more times. Narrow by one notch and repeat the fold-and-pass, then narrow once more and repeat again. From there, step down one setting per pass until the sheet is delicate, elastic, and slightly translucent — setting 6 for fettuccine, one notch thinner for ravioli. Lay the sheet on the floured parchment, folding with flour or parchment between layers, and cover. Repeat with the remaining quarters.
  6. 10 minutesCut the sheets into 12-14 inch segments and feed each through the cutter attachment, or slice by hand with a chef's knife. Divide into portions, dust with flour, curl into nests on the parchment-lined sheet, and cover with a towel.
  7. 5 minutesBring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil. Add the pasta, stir gently, and cook, tasting as you go, until just set with a definite bite — 1½ to 2 minutes. Don't go past 2 minutes or it turns mushy. Drain, toss with sauce, serve.

Notes

  • Laminating (the fold-and-repass in step 5) is what patches holes and evens out the sheet. Feed the folded dough back in at a rotated angle, and always dial back to the widest setting before the first pass of a fold — otherwise the thick packet jams the rollers.
  • Fresh pasta firms up slightly in the first seconds of cooking, so undercooking leaves it pasty. Taste rather than trusting the clock.
  • Tweaks: an extra yolk plus a little more flour gives a richer, more golden dough; a teaspoon of oil gives softer, mushier noodles. 00 flour makes silkier sheets, semolina a rougher surface that grabs sauce.

Storage

  • Dough: wrap in plastic and refrigerate — it works fine but gradually greys, which doesn't affect flavor or texture. For longer, seal the wrapped ball in a zipper bag with the air pressed out and freeze up to 3 weeks; thaw in the fridge until soft and pliant.
  • Cut noodles: freeze the nests on the baking sheet, then transfer to a zipper-lock bag for up to 3 weeks. Cook straight from the freezer.
