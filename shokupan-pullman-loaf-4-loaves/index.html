Yeasted shokupan pullman loaf (4 loaves)

Bread

Doubled batch adapted for a 4:30 PM start and only 2 pullman pans (baked in two sequential batches, second batch cold-retarded).

Ingredients

Tangzhong

  • 100 g bread flour
  • 500 g whole milk

Main dough

  • 1400 grams bread flour
  • 220 grams granulated sugar
  • 8 teaspoon (32 g) diamond kosher crystal salt
  • 4 packets (24 g) active dry yeast
  • 32 g milk powder
  • 4 large egg
  • 480 g milk
  • 180 g (12 tablespoons) unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  • ~600 g tangzhong

Egg Wash

  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • Pinch of salt

Directions

Example schedule

  • 4:30 PM: Make the tangzhong
  • 5:30 PM: Mix dough
  • 5:45 PM: Add butter and mix
  • 6:05 PM: Bulk rise ~1 hour
  • 7:15 PM: Divide into 12 balls → batch 1 into the 2 pans; batch 2 onto a tray in the fridge
  • 7:35 PM: Preheat oven to 350°F
  • 7:55 PM: Bake batch 1 (30–35 min)
  • 8:30 PM: Turn batch 1 out onto a rack; re-oil the pans
  • 8:35 PM: Transfer cold batch 2 into the pans; proof ~50–60 min
  • 9:30 PM: Bake batch 2 (30–35 min)
  • 10:05 PM: Turn out, cool
  • Eat: batch 1 ~9:00 PM; batch 2 late evening or hold for the next morning

Make the tangzhong (4:30 PM)

Whisk 100 g flour and 500 g whole milk together until it achieves a smooth consistency. Pour into a non-stick pan and heat over medium heat, stirring constantly until it achieves the consistency of thin mashed potatoes. A spatula should leave a trail. If you have a thermometer, it will be about 155 F degrees. Let cool for 30 to 45 minutes in the refrigerator until the mixture is room temperature.

Mix (5:30 PM)

Whisk the dry ingredients (1400 g bread flour, 220 g granulated sugar, 8 teaspoon (32 g) DCK salt, 4 packets (24 g) active dry yeast, 32 g milk powder) in the bowl of a stand mixer. Add 4 eggs, 480 g milk, and the tangzhong, with paddle attachment, until completely combined. Let rest for 10 minutes. Switch to dough hook attachment. Add the butter pieces, one every few seconds, and mix on medium speed for 15 to 20 minutes. Dough should be very stretchy. Let rise for about 1 hour in an oiled bowl until doubled in size. This should take about an hour at 75F°.

Mixer capacity: 1400 g of flour is a lot of dough — a standard 5–6 qt home stand mixer will struggle with the full batch and may overheat. If yours is standard-sized, mix in two batches (or knead by hand). Rise/proof/bake times are unaffected.

Shape — two batches (7:15 PM)

Divide the dough into twelve balls.

  • Batch 1: arrange three balls each in your 2 oiled 9 inch by 5 inch pans, cover with plastic wrap, and allow to rise about 40 minutes, until it is about 3/4 of the height of the pan.
  • Batch 2: place the remaining six balls onto a floured, covered tray and put them straight into the refrigerator to retard while batch 1 proofs and bakes.

Preheat oven to 350°F.

Bake batch 1 (7:55 PM)

Brush the loaves with the egg wash (if not using pullman lids) and bake for 30 to 35 minutes at 350°F. Remove the loaves from the pans and cool on a wire rack. Re-oil the pans.

Bake batch 2 (8:35 PM)

Transfer the cold batch 2 balls into the 2 pans. Because the dough is cold, proof ~50–60 minutes (longer than batch 1) until about 3/4 of the height of the pan. Brush with egg wash and bake for 30 to 35 minutes at 350°F. Remove from the pans and cool completely on a wire rack.

Flexibility: the fridge retard is forgiving — if batch 1 runs long, batch 2 can stay chilled longer without harm. To avoid a late night, retard batch 2's dough overnight and bake it fresh in the morning (~1 hour to warm up and proof, then bake).
