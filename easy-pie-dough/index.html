Easy Pie Dough

Dessert

Kenji López-Alt's easy food-processor pie dough, from Serious Eats. Instead of cutting butter into all the flour, two thirds of the flour is fully blended with the butter into a paste, then the rest is pulsed in just until broken up. That makes a dough that is flaky, tender and very hard to mess up. Makes enough for one double-crust or two single-crust 9-inch pies.

Makes: one double-crust or two single-crust 9-inch piesEstimated Total Time: ~2 3/4 hours (mostly chilling)

Ingredients

  • 350 grams all-purpose flour (2 3/4 cups; 12.5 oz), divided into 235g and 115g
  • 25 grams sugar (2 tbsp)
  • 5 grams Diamond Crystal kosher salt (1 1/2 tsp; for table salt, use the same weight or half the volume)
  • 280 grams unsalted butter (2 1/2 sticks; 10 oz), cut into 1/4-inch-thick slices
  • 85 grams cold water (6 tbsp)

Directions

  1. 5 minutesCombine 235g flour, 25g sugar and 5g salt in the bowl of a food processor. Pulse twice to incorporate. Spread the 280g butter slices evenly over the surface. Pulse until no dry flour remains and the dough just begins to collect in clumps, about 25 short pulses. Use a rubber spatula to spread the dough evenly around the bowl. Sprinkle with the remaining 115g flour and pulse until the dough is just barely broken up, about 5 short pulses. Transfer to a large bowl.
  2. 5 minutesthen chill 2 hoursSprinkle with 85g cold water. Using a rubber spatula, fold and press the dough until it comes together into a ball. Divide in half, form each half into a 4-inch disk, wrap tightly in plastic and refrigerate for at least 2 hours.
  3. 10 minutesPull out one disk, set it on a well-floured work surface and sprinkle with more flour. Use a tapered rolling pin to roll it into a circle, lifting and rotating the dough as you go to keep it even. Keep rolling, changing the angle of the pin to get an even shape and thickness. The finished round should overhang your pie plate by an inch or two.
  4. 5 minutesPick up the dough by carefully rolling it around the rolling pin, using a bench scraper to help lift it off the work surface. Unroll it over the pie plate. Gently lift and fit the dough into the plate, getting down into the corners.
  5. 5 minutesFor a single-crust pie, trim the dough with scissors to a 1/2-inch overhang all around. For a double-crust pie, fill it now, roll out the second disk and drape it over the top, then trim both crusts together to a 1/2-inch overhang. Either way, tuck the overhang under itself all the way around.
  6. 5 minutesFlute the edges using the forefinger of one hand and the thumb and forefinger of the other. A single-crust shell is now ready to blind-bake or fill. For a double-crust pie, brush the top with egg white, sprinkle with sugar and cut vent holes with a sharp knife before baking.

Full example schedule

  1. 1:00 PM: Make the dough in the food processor
  2. 1:05 PM: Add water, form two disks, refrigerate
  3. 3:10 PM: Roll out the first disk
  4. 3:20 PM: Transfer to the pie plate
  5. 3:25 PM: Trim (fill and top for a double crust)
  6. 3:30 PM: Flute; blind-bake, fill or bake
