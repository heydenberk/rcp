Milk Bread Dinner Rolls

Bread

A richer version of the shokupan tangzhong dough, shaped into pull-apart dinner rolls. Compared with the loaf it has more butter (20% of the flour instead of 12%) and two extra egg yolks, with a little less milk to balance the added liquid. The tangzhong keeps the rolls soft for a day or two, so they can be baked the day before and rewarmed. Makes 24 rolls in two 9x13 pans. About 4½ hours from making the tangzhong to rolls out of the oven.

Ingredients

Tangzhong

  • 50 grams bread flour
  • 250 grams whole milk

Main dough

  • 700 grams bread flour
  • 90 grams granulated sugar
  • 13 grams Diamond Crystal kosher salt (about 4¾ tsp)
  • 10 grams active dry yeast (about 3 tsp)
  • 32 grams milk powder
  • 2 large eggs plus 2 large egg yolks
  • 210 grams whole milk
  • 150 grams unsalted butter (10½ tbsp), cut into pieces and softened
  • ~300 grams tangzhong

Finishing

  • 1 egg, beaten with 1 tbsp milk and a pinch of salt, for egg wash
  • 30 grams unsalted butter (2 tbsp), melted, plus more for the pans
  • Flaky salt (optional)

Directions

  1. 45 minutesTangzhong: Whisk 50g bread flour and 250g whole milk until smooth. Cook in a nonstick pan over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it thickens to the consistency of thin mashed potatoes and a spatula leaves a trail, about 155°F. Weigh it and top it back up to 300g with milk if some evaporated. Press plastic wrap onto the surface and cool in the fridge 30–45 minutes, to room temperature.
  2. 15 minutesMix: Whisk 700g bread flour, 90g sugar, 13g salt, 10g active dry yeast, and 32g milk powder in the bowl of a stand mixer. Add the 2 eggs, 2 yolks, 210g milk, and the tangzhong, and mix with the paddle until combined. Rest 10 minutes.
  3. 20 minutesKnead: Switch to the dough hook and knead 4–6 minutes on medium-low (KitchenAid speed 2–4) until smooth and starting to get stretchy. Add the 150g butter a piece at a time over 4–5 minutes, letting each piece mostly disappear before the next. There is more butter than in the loaf, so the dough will look greasy and broken for a while before it comes back together. Knead until very stretchy (windowpane), about 10–12 more minutes. Aim for a final dough temperature of about 78°F.
  4. 75–90 minutesBulk rise: Cover in an oiled bowl and let rise until doubled, 75–90 minutes at about 75°F. The extra butter and yolks make it a little slower than the loaf.
  5. 20 minutesShape: Butter two 9x13 metal pans. Divide the dough into 24 pieces (~68g each). Round each into a tight ball by cupping your hand over it on an unfloured counter and moving it in small circles until the surface is taut. Arrange 12 per pan, seam side down, in a 3x4 grid.
  6. 45–60 minutesProof: Cover and let rise 45–60 minutes, until puffy and touching each other, and a gentle poke springs back slowly. Preheat the oven to 350°F with racks in the upper and lower thirds about 30 minutes before baking.
  7. 25 minutesBake: Brush the rolls gently with the egg wash. Bake 20–25 minutes, swapping and rotating the pans halfway through, until deep golden brown and 190°F in the center of a middle roll. Tent with foil if they brown too fast.
  8. 10 minutesFinish: Brush the hot rolls with the 30g melted butter and sprinkle with flaky salt if using. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then lift them out onto a rack.

Make ahead

  • Baking the day before: Let the rolls cool completely (about 1 hour), then wrap the pans tightly in foil or bag the slabs of rolls. Keep them at room temperature; the fridge makes them stale faster. To serve, rewarm covered in foil at 300°F for 8–10 minutes.
  • Baking the next day: After shaping in step 5, cover the pans tightly and refrigerate overnight (up to 16 hours). The next day, let them sit at room temperature 1–1½ hours, until puffy, then egg wash and bake as in step 7.
  • Freezing: Wrap the cooled, baked rolls tightly and freeze for up to a month. Thaw at room temperature, still wrapped, then rewarm as above.

Full example schedule

  1. 9:00 AM: Make tangzhong; cool
  2. 9:45 AM: Mix dough; rest 10 minutes
  3. 10:00 AM: Knead, add butter, knead to windowpane
  4. 10:20 AM: Bulk rise (75–90 min)
  5. 11:45 AM: Divide into 24 and shape
  6. 12:05 PM: Proof (45–60 min); preheat oven to 350°F at about 12:30
  7. 1:00 PM: Egg wash and bake (20–25 min)
  8. 1:25 PM: Brush with butter, cool
