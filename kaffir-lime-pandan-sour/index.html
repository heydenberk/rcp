Kaffir Lime Pandan Sour

Drink

Kaffir lime and pandan sour, batched for a party. Super juice stands in for fresh lime 1:1, kaffir leaf is infused into the rum, and the whole thing is stripped down to two jugs and no ice bath — a frozen bottle of rum chills the batch harder than a sink full of ice would. Built around one 750 ml bottle of Smith & Cross. Makes 16 drinks (eight rounds of two) plus the test drink. About 20 minutes of actual work.

Ingredients

Kaffir tincture

  • 100 ml Smith & Cross, poured off the bottle before it goes in the freezer
  • 4 leaves kaffir lime (they grow as figure-8 double lobes — 4 pairs, 8 lobes)

Pandan mix (sugar, water and pandan in one jug)

  • 280 grams sugar
  • 575 ml cold water — i.e. topped up to 750 ml total, not plus 750 ml
  • 1/2 tsp pandan extract, to start

Batch

  • 750 ml Smith & Cross Traditional Jamaica Rum (one bottle, all of it — tincture included)
  • 375 ml super lime juice
  • 750 ml pandan mix (all of the above)
  • 6 grams fine sea salt (1 tsp)

Per round

  • 500 ml egg whites (1 fl oz / 30 g per drink — about 16 whites)
  • 1 whole nutmeg, for grating
  • 2 leaves kaffir lime, for garnish

You will need: a shaker, a fine mesh sieve, a squeeze bottle or small pitcher for the whites, a 2-litre pitcher, and plenty of ice for shaking.

Directions

  1. 5 minutesTincture, then the bottle into the freezer. Pour 100 ml off the bottle. Tear the woody center spine out of 4 kaffir leaves and discard it, tear the lobes, crush them in your palm — you should smell them immediately — and drop them into the 100 ml. Cover, leave at room temperature. The rest of the bottle goes in the freezer and stays there until service; at 57% it won't freeze, and it's what makes the ice bath unnecessary.
  2. 10 minutesPandan mix. Put the 280 g sugar in a jug or bottle, add 575 ml cold water, seal and shake. (The sugar itself adds about 175 ml of volume once dissolved, so you're topping up to 750 ml total — not adding 750 ml of water to the sugar.) It looks like wet sand for a minute, then goes clear — 5 minutes of intermittent shaking, no heat needed. Dose with 1/2 tsp pandan extract, taste, and go up in 1/8 tsp increments. It should read noticeably too strong: it gets diluted about two and a half times in the finished batch. Push harder than you would with a clear base — Smith & Cross's esters compete with pandan — but stop before it turns soapy. Into the fridge.
  3. 10 minutesEgg whites. Measure 500 ml and pass it once through a fine sieve into a squeeze bottle or small pitcher — something you can pour from accurately without looking. Fridge. (Whites you separated today don't need the stirring that pooled or carton whites do; the sieve is just catching chalazae.)
  4. 5 minutes, at the 45-minute markTaste and strain the tincture. At 30 minutes taste it — it should be aggressively, almost unpleasantly perfumed, since it gets diluted about nineteen times over. What you're watching for is the tip from lime-blossom into soap or pine cleaner; if it's crossed that line, strain immediately and use half, topping back up with plain rum. Strain out the leaves by 45 minutes either way.
  5. 10 minutesCombine, 15 minutes before service. Into the pitcher: the frozen rum, the strained tincture, 375 ml super lime juice, the cold pandan mix and 6 g salt. Stir. It lands around −5°C straight away — the frozen rum is nearly half the volume and drags everything down with it. Do this last, not early, so nothing has time to warm up. Pour a test drink, shake it with a splash of egg white and taste: if it reads flabby add a splash more super juice (you have ~25 ml spare); if it reads harsh, a little more sugar. Coupes into the freezer.
  6. 1 minuteService — dry shake. Per round of two drinks: 7.5 oz batch plus 2 oz egg white into the shaker, no ice. Seal and shake hard, holding the seal with your palm — egg white expands under pressure and will pop a lazy seal.
  7. 1 minuteService — shake with ice. Open, fill with ice, seal again, and shake until the tin is painfully cold and frosted over.
  8. 1 minutethen 30 seconds~3 minutes per round, eight roundsService — strain, rest, garnish. Double-strain through the built-in strainer plus a fine mesh sieve into the chilled coupes. Let them sit 30 seconds so the foam sets, then grate nutmeg over the top and scatter a few threads of finely chiffonaded kaffir leaf — slap the leaf between your palms first, like mint.

The color lands olive-khaki, not jade. The white cap still looks sharp against it.

Notes

On super juice. It's acid-adjusted to fresh lime strength — about 6% — so it swaps 1:1 with no recalculation, which is why there's no citric acid anywhere in this recipe. It's also better than an acid solution here: the peel extraction gives it real lime aromatics, where straight citric acid is sour but hollow. And unlike fresh juice it keeps for weeks, so there's no 8-hour freshness window to race.

Why there's no ice bath. The batch needs to be cold going into the shaker, or it picks up extra melt reaching drinking temperature and over-dilutes. An ice bath is one way; a frozen bottle of rum is easier and works better. At 750 ml against 1125 ml of fridge-cold mix, the rum pulls the blend to about −5°C on contact.

Combining early instead. Nothing here is time-sensitive once the tincture is strained, so you can build the batch hours ahead — it just needs a different cold source, since the frozen-rum trick only pays off if you combine late. The fridge gets you to about 4°C, which is what an ice bath would have managed. The freezer is better still: at roughly 23% ABV with this much dissolved sugar the batch freezes around −13°C, so a couple of hours gets it to about −10°C without slushing. Stir before the first round; sugar-heavy batches stratify a little when they get that cold. What doesn't work is combining early and leaving it on the counter.

Don't loose-steep the leaves in the batch. Sitting in the full pitcher they'll run past bright into soapy with no way back. Infusing 100 ml hard, tasting, and straining means the one irreversible mistake can only happen in a tenth of the drink.

If you have 1:1 simple syrup instead of sugar, use 500 ml of it in place of the 280 g, plus 250 ml water for the same 750 ml total — and skip the shaking, since there's nothing to dissolve.

On the egg whites. Weight and volume are interchangeable — egg white is close enough to water in density that 1 fl oz and 30 g are the same thing. Whites left over from a same-day bake are better than pooled ones: they haven't had time to stratify into thick and thin albumen, so they whip to a firmer foam.

Why 1 oz and not 0.75. Bar spec is usually 0.75 oz for a tighter foam, but this recipe is written for whites that may have been sitting. Fresh ones will foam a little more enthusiastically — no harm, just expect a taller cap.

Two drinks per shaker is the ceiling. Three and the foam collapses. Eight rounds at roughly three minutes each is about 25 minutes of actual shaking spread across the evening.

The ratio. 6 parts rum : 3 parts super juice : 6 parts pandan mix, by volume; the pandan mix is 280 g sugar made up to 750 ml. Salt is 1 g per 125 ml of rum. Drinks land at one per 111 ml of batch.

Full example schedule

  1. 3:00 PM: Pour off 100 ml rum, start the kaffir tincture, bottle into the freezer
  2. 3:05 PM: Shake up the pandan mix, dose the pandan, into the fridge
  3. 3:20 PM: Measure and sieve the egg whites
  4. 3:45 PM: Taste and strain the tincture
  5. 4:30 PM: Combine rum, tincture, super juice, pandan mix and salt; test drink; coupes into the freezer
  6. 4:45 PM: Service — dry shake, shake with ice, double-strain, rest 30 seconds, nutmeg and kaffir
  7. Repeat every ~3 minutes
