Pandan Sour For A Crowd

Drink

A pandan sour engineered for a dinner party: everything batched ahead, egg white added per round. Built around one 750 ml bottle of Smith & Cross — the whole bottle goes in, nothing measured, nothing left. Makes 16 drinks (eight rounds of two) plus the test drink you pour before service. About three hours start to finish, with no overnight step.

Ingredients

Pandan syrup

  • 280 grams sugar
  • 330 ml cold water
  • 1/2 tsp pandan extract, to start (see notes)

Batch (everything but the egg white)

  • 750 ml Smith & Cross Traditional Jamaica Rum (one bottle, all of it)
  • 250 ml ice-cold water
  • 375 ml fresh lime juice (about 12 to 14 limes)
  • 500 ml pandan syrup (all of the above)
  • 6 grams fine sea salt (1 tsp)

Per round

  • 500 ml pooled egg whites (1 fl oz / 30 g per drink — about 16 whites)
  • 1 whole nutmeg, for grating

You will need: a shaker, a fine mesh sieve, a squeeze bottle or small pitcher for the whites, a bowl or sink big enough to ice-bath a 2-litre pitcher, and 16 coupes (or fewer, washed between rounds).

Directions

  1. 1 minuteRum into the freezer. Do this before anything else. It's 40% of the batch by volume and at 57% ABV it won't freeze, so it's free chilling that runs while you work.
  2. 15 minutesPandan syrup, cold. Put the 280g sugar and 330ml cold water in a sealed bottle or jar and shake. It'll look like wet sand for a minute, then go clear — total 5 to 10 minutes of intermittent shaking, no heat needed. Dose with 1/2 tsp pandan extract, taste, and go up in 1/8 tsp increments. Push harder than you would with a clear base — Smith & Cross's esters compete with pandan, and you're fighting amber on color. Stop before it turns soapy.
  3. 20 minutesJuice the limes. Juice 12 to 14 limes to get 375 ml, and fine-strain. Doing this a few hours out is if anything better than the morning of — citrus starts tasting flat after about 8 hours.
  4. 35 minutesBuild the batch and ice-bath it. In a pitcher, combine the whole bottle of rum (out of the freezer), 250ml ice-cold water, 375ml lime juice, 500ml pandan syrup and 6g fine sea salt. Stir well. Then sit the pitcher in a bowl or sink of ice, water and a couple of big handfuls of salt, and stir it every few minutes until it's properly cold — about 25 minutes. Then into the fridge.
  5. 10 minutesPrep the whites. Stir or briefly whisk the container first (see notes), then measure 500 ml and pass it through a fine sieve into a squeeze bottle or small pitcher — something you can pour accurately from without looking. Back in the fridge.
  6. 15 minutesTaste and chill glasses. Pour one drink's worth of batch, shake it with a little egg white, and taste. If it reads flabby, stir another 1/4 oz lime per 6 drinks into the pitcher. Better to find this out now than mid-service. Coupes into the freezer.
  7. 1 minuteService — dry shake. Per round of two drinks: 7.5 oz batch plus 2 oz egg white into the shaker, no ice. Seal and shake hard, holding the seal with your palm — egg white expands under pressure and will pop a lazy seal.
  8. 1 minuteService — shake with ice. Open, fill with ice, seal again, and shake until the tin is painfully cold and frosted over.
  9. 1 minutethen 30 seconds~3 minutes per round, eight roundsService — strain, rest, nutmeg. Double-strain through the built-in strainer plus a fine mesh sieve into the chilled coupes. Let them sit 30 seconds so the foam sets, then grate nutmeg over the top. Start the next round while these rest.

The color lands olive-khaki, not jade. The white cap still looks sharp against it.

If you find arrack instead of Smith & Cross, don't use these ratios — go to 2 oz spirit, no water, 3/4 oz syrup per drink.

Notes

The ratio, if your bottle isn't 750 ml. The batch is 6 parts rum : 2 parts water : 3 parts lime : 4 parts pandan syrup, by volume. Divide your bottle by six and multiply out — a 700 ml bottle gives 233 / 350 / 467, a 1 litre bottle gives 333 / 500 / 667. Salt is 1 g per 125 ml of rum. Drinks come out at one per 111 ml of finished batch.

Why the syrup doesn't need heat. Heat is only a shortcut for dissolving sugar fast, and at this ratio you don't need it: 280 g in 330 ml is well under what water will hold at room temperature, so it dissolves cold with nothing but agitation. Skipping the heat also removes the only real reason this recipe used to want a day's lead time — you have to taste the pandan dose cold, because a syrup dosed while hot reads completely differently once chilled. Cold-mixed, it's at tasting temperature immediately. The syrup gains nothing from sitting overnight. It keeps a month in the fridge, so make extra if you like.

Getting the batch cold is the real constraint. With an overnight schedule the fridge does this for free. Compressed, it won't — a near-2-litre pitcher takes hours to come down in a fridge, and a warm batch picks up extra melt in the shaker to reach the same temperature, which over-dilutes the drink. Hence the freezer trick in step 1 and the ice bath in step 4. Salt in the bath matters: it takes the water below 0°C and roughly halves the time.

On the egg whites. Pooled whites stratify in the container and the thick and thin albumen separate, so a lazy pour off the top gives you weak foam. Stir or briefly whisk before measuring, and sieve once. Weight and volume are interchangeable here — egg white is close enough to water in density that 1 fl oz and 30 g are the same thing. If they've been refrigerated more than about four days, smell them before you commit.

Why 1 oz and not 0.75. Bar spec is usually 0.75 oz for a tighter foam, but separated whites that have sat have a broken protein network and don't set up as firmly. The extra volume buys that back.

Pacing. Roughly three minutes a round, so six guests with one drink each is about ten minutes of actual work. The full bottle is eight rounds — figure five or six guests drinking freely.

Full example schedule

  1. 5:00 PM: Rum into the freezer
  2. 5:05 PM: Cold-shake the pandan syrup, dose to taste
  3. 5:20 PM: Juice and strain the limes
  4. 5:45 PM: Build the batch, ice-bath it cold, then into the fridge
  5. 6:20 PM: Stir, measure and sieve the egg whites into a squeeze bottle
  6. 7:30 PM: Taste a test drink, adjust lime if needed, coupes into the freezer
  7. 8:00 PM: Service — dry shake, shake with ice, double-strain, rest 30 seconds, nutmeg
  8. Repeat step 7 every ~3 minutes
