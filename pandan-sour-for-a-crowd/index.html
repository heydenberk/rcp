Pandan Sour For A Crowd

A pandan sour engineered for a dinner party: everything batched ahead, egg white added per round. Makes 20 drinks — a 75 fl oz batch at 3.75 fl oz per drink, plus 1 oz egg white each. Two drinks per shaker is the hard ceiling, so that's ten rounds. Start the pandan syrup the day before.

Ingredients

Pandan syrup (make the day before)

  • 330 grams sugar (1 2/3 cups)
  • 395 ml hot water (1 2/3 cups)
  • 1/2 tsp pandan extract, to start (see notes)

Batch (everything but the egg white)

  • 890 ml Smith & Cross Traditional Jamaica Rum (30 fl oz — a bottle and a bit)
  • 295 ml cold water (10 fl oz)
  • 445 ml fresh lime juice (15 fl oz — about 15 to 18 limes)
  • 590 ml pandan syrup (20 fl oz — all of the above)
  • 7 grams fine sea salt (1 1/4 tsp)

Per round

  • 590 ml pooled egg whites (20 fl oz total — 1 fl oz / 30 g per drink)
  • 1 whole nutmeg, for grating

You will need: a shaker, a fine mesh sieve, a squeeze bottle or small pitcher for the whites, and 20 coupes (or fewer, washed between rounds).

Directions

  1. 20 minutesthen overnightthe day beforeDay before — pandan syrup. Dissolve the 330g sugar in the 395ml hot water, then cool it completely. Start with 1/2 tsp pandan extract, taste, and go up in 1/8 tsp increments. Push harder than you would with a clear base — Smith & Cross's esters compete with pandan, and you're fighting amber on color. Stop before it turns soapy. Refrigerate. Taste it cold and adjust then: a syrup dosed while hot reads completely differently once chilled.
  2. 25 minutesMorning of — juice the limes. Juice 15 to 18 limes to get 445 ml, and fine-strain. This is the one thing that can't be done the night before — citrus starts tasting flat after about 8 hours.
  3. 10 minutesMorning of — build the batch. In a pitcher or bottle, combine the 890ml rum, 295ml cold water, 445ml lime juice, 590ml pandan syrup and 7g fine sea salt. Stir well and refrigerate. Everything except the egg white — the batch is stable all day.
  4. 10 minutesMorning of — prep the whites. Stir or briefly whisk the container first (see notes), then measure 590 ml and pass it through a fine sieve into a squeeze bottle or small pitcher — something you can pour accurately from without looking. Back in the fridge.
  5. 15 minutesBefore guests — taste and chill glasses. Pour one drink's worth of batch, shake it with a little egg white, and taste. If it reads flabby, stir another 1/4 oz lime per 6 drinks into the pitcher. Better to find this out now than mid-service. Coupes into the freezer.
  6. 1 minuteService — dry shake. Per round of two drinks: 7.5 oz batch plus 2 oz egg white into the shaker, no ice. Seal and shake hard, holding the seal with your palm — egg white expands under pressure and will pop a lazy seal.
  7. 1 minuteService — shake with ice. Open, fill with ice, seal again, and shake until the tin is painfully cold and frosted over.
  8. 1 minutethen 30 seconds~3 minutes per round, ten roundsService — strain, rest, nutmeg. Double-strain through the built-in strainer plus a fine mesh sieve into the chilled coupes. Let them sit 30 seconds so the foam sets, then grate nutmeg over the top. Start the next round while these rest.

The color lands olive-khaki, not jade. The white cap still looks sharp against it.

If you find arrack instead of Smith & Cross, don't use these ratios — go to 2 oz spirit, no water, 3/4 oz syrup per drink.

Notes

On the egg whites. Pooled whites stratify in the container and the thick and thin albumen separate, so a lazy pour off the top gives you weak foam. Stir or briefly whisk before measuring, and sieve once. Weight and volume are interchangeable here — egg white is close enough to water in density that 1 fl oz and 30 g are the same thing. If they've been refrigerated more than about four days, smell them before you commit.

Why 1 oz and not 0.75. Bar spec is usually 0.75 oz for a tighter foam, but separated whites that have sat have a broken protein network and don't set up as firmly. The extra volume buys that back.

On the syrup quantity. A 1:1 cup of sugar to a cup of water makes about 12 oz of syrup, enough for 12 drinks; the quantities above are that scaled up to cover all 20. It keeps a month in the fridge, so making extra is cheap insurance.

Pacing. Roughly three minutes a round, so six guests with one drink each is about ten minutes of actual work. The full batch is ten rounds — figure six or seven guests drinking freely.

Full example schedule

  1. 6:00 PM, day before: Make the pandan syrup, cool, dose to taste, refrigerate
  2. 10:00 AM: Juice and strain the limes
  3. 10:30 AM: Build the batch, refrigerate
  4. 11:00 AM: Stir, measure and sieve the egg whites into a squeeze bottle
  5. 7:30 PM: Taste a test drink, adjust lime if needed, coupes into the freezer
  6. 8:00 PM: Service — dry shake, shake with ice, double-strain, rest 30 seconds, nutmeg
  7. Repeat step 6 every ~3 minutes
