Same-Day Round Pizza

Main

The quick, warm-water dough from the 9x13 pan pizza, adjusted for hand-stretched rounds: bread flour, less water, and less oil so it stretches thin without tearing and bakes up crisp and chewy. Baked on a preheated steel or stone. Makes five 12-inch pizzas. About 2¾ hours from mixing to the first pizza, and 3½ hours to the last.

Ingredients

  • 780 grams bread flour (6 cups), or see Flour options for 00
  • 510 grams warm water, 105–110°F (2⅛ cups)
  • 18 grams Diamond Crystal kosher salt (1¾ tbsp)
  • 20 grams olive oil, for the dough (1½ tbsp)
  • 8 grams sugar (2 tsp)
  • 7 grams instant yeast (2¼ tsp)
  • Olive oil, for the bowl and dough balls
  • Semolina or flour for the peel (or parchment)
  • Sauce, cheese, and toppings

Directions

  1. 5 minutesIn a large bowl, combine 780g bread flour, 18g Diamond Crystal kosher salt, 8g sugar, and 7g instant yeast. Whisk thoroughly so the salt and yeast are evenly distributed.
  2. 5 minutesAdd 510g warm (~107°F) water and 20g olive oil. Stir until a shaggy, cohesive dough forms.
  3. 10 minutesKnead by hand or in a mixer 8–10 minutes, until smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky. This dough is stiffer than the pan pizza dough, so it should not stick to your hands.
  4. 90 minutesForm the dough into a ball, lightly oil the bowl, cover, and let rise in a warm spot ~90 minutes, until roughly doubled in size.
  5. About an hour before baking, put a steel or stone on a rack in the upper third of the oven and preheat to the oven's highest setting (500–550°F). The steel needs the full hour to heat through. (4:20 PM)
  6. 30–45 minutesTurn the dough out and divide into 5 equal pieces (~265g each). Shape each into a tight ball, lightly oil, and set on a tray or in individual containers. Cover and rest 30–45 minutes, until puffy and relaxed.
  7. 3 minutesFlour the counter and a dough ball. Press the center out with your fingertips, leaving a 1-inch rim, then stretch over your knuckles to 12 inches. If it springs back, let it rest a few minutes and continue.
  8. 2 minutesLay the dough on a peel dusted with semolina (or on parchment). Top lightly; heavy or wet toppings will keep the center from crisping. Shake the peel to make sure the pizza slides freely.
  9. 6–12 minutesSlide onto the steel and bake 6–8 minutes on a steel or 8–12 minutes on a stone, until the rim is puffed and browned and the bottom is spotted. If the top lags behind the bottom, switch to the broiler for the last minute.
  10. ~12 minutes per pizza5:30–Rest a minute, then slice. Give the steel 3–5 minutes to recover between pizzas while you stretch the next one.

Flour options

Italian 00 flour is milled finer and makes a softer, more tender crust than bread flour. Most 00 is made for wood-fired ovens at 800°F and up. It has no malted barley added, so in a home oven it browns slowly and can bake up pale and crackery. The yeast, timings, and fridge options stay the same for every option below.

  • Half bread flour, half 00 (home oven): Use 390g bread flour and 390g 00, and keep everything else the same. This is the easiest way to get a softer, more Neapolitan-like crust that still browns well at 550°F.
  • All 00 (home oven): Use 780g 00 and cut the water to 490 grams (63%), since 00 absorbs less water than bread flour. Keep the sugar and oil, which help it brown. Bake on the steel in the upper third of the oven and finish under the broiler for the last 1–2 minutes. A 00 sold for home or deck ovens browns better than one sold for wood-fired ovens (Caputo Pizzeria, for example).
  • All 00 (outdoor pizza oven, 800–900°F): Use 780g 00 and 480 grams water (62%), and leave out the sugar and oil, which burn at these temperatures. Make the balls a little smaller (~250g) for 10–11 inch pizzas, and bake 60–90 seconds, turning often.

Fridge retardation

The dough holds well in the fridge, which makes it easy to work ahead or to push dinner back. With this much yeast, it gets past its best after about a day, so for longer holds cut the yeast.

  • How to store: Put each oiled ball in its own lidded container (a quart deli container works), or space them on a lightly oiled tray covered tightly with plastic. Leave room for the balls to double.
  • Planned overnight: Shorten the bulk rise to about 45 minutes, then divide, ball, and refrigerate right away. They hold for up to 24 hours.
  • Planned 2–3 days out: Cut the yeast to 3 grams (¾ tsp), skip the bulk rise, and divide, ball, and refrigerate right after kneading. They hold for 48–72 hours, and the flavor improves.
  • Plans changed mid-day: Refrigerate the balls at any point after step 6. If they have already rested and puffed, use them within about 12 hours.
  • Plans changed during bulk: Refrigerate the whole covered bowl. When you're ready, divide the cold dough, ball it, and let the balls rest 1½–2 hours at room temperature before stretching.
  • Taking them out: Let the balls sit covered at room temperature 1–2 hours, until they are no longer cold in the center and feel soft and puffy. Cold dough is stiff, springs back, and tears when you stretch it. Preheat the steel for the last hour.
  • If they overproofed: The balls will have spread flat and run together, with large surface bubbles and a boozy smell. Stretch them gently on an oiled counter instead of a floured one, because they will be slack and tear easily, and expect a flatter rim. Badly overproofed dough is still good pressed into an oiled 9x13 pan and baked as pan pizza.

Full example schedule

  1. 3:00 PM: Mix dry ingredients
  2. 3:05 PM: Add water and oil
  3. 3:10 PM: Knead (8–10 min)
  4. 3:20 PM: Bulk rise (~90 min)
  5. 4:20 PM: Preheat oven and steel at max temperature
  6. 4:50 PM: Divide into 5 balls and rest (30–45 min)
  7. 5:30 PM: Stretch, top, and bake the first pizza (6–8 min)
  8. 5:30–6:30 PM: Keep going, one pizza every ~12 minutes
