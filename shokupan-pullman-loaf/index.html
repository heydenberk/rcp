Yeasted shokupan pullman loaf

Makes one 13x4x4 lidded Pullman loaf, or two open-top 9x5 loaves.

Ingredients

Tangzhong

  • 50 g bread flour
  • 250 g whole milk

Main dough

  • 700 grams bread flour
  • 90 grams granulated sugar
  • 15 g (about 5 1/2 teaspoons) diamond crystal kosher salt
  • 10 g (about 3 teaspoons) active dry yeast
  • 16 g milk powder
  • 2 large egg
  • 240 g milk
  • 90 g (6 tablespoons) unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  • ~300 g tangzhong

Egg Wash (open-top 9x5 loaves only — skip for lidded Pullman)

  • 1 egg (you'll only use about half; a beaten half egg is plenty)
  • 1 tablespoon water
  • Pinch of salt

Directions

Example schedule

  • 9:00 AM: Make the tangzhong
  • 10:00 AM: Mix dough
  • 10:45 AM: Bulk rise (~1 hour)
  • 11:45 AM: Shape dough
  • 11:55 AM: Preheat oven to 350°F
  • 12:25 PM: Bake (dough proofed ~40 minutes)
  • ~1:05 PM: Out of the oven; cool on a rack
  • 3:30 PM: Eat — shokupan needs 2 to 3 hours of cooling before slicing, or the crumb compresses into gumminess

Make the tangzhong (9 AM)

Whisk 50 g flour and 250 g whole milk together until it achieves a smooth consistency. Pour into a non-stick pan and heat over medium heat, stirring constantly until it achieves the consistency of thin mashed potatoes. A spatula should leave a trail. If you have a thermometer, it will be about 155 F degrees. Let cool for 30 to 45 minutes in the refrigerator until the mixture is room temperature.

Mix (10 AM)

Whisk the dry ingredients (700 g bread flour, 90 g granulated sugar, 15 g DCK salt, 10 g active dry yeast, 16 g milk powder) in the bowl of a stand mixer. Add 2 eggs, 240 g milk, and the tangzhong; mix with the paddle attachment until completely combined. Let rest for 10 minutes.

Switch to the dough hook and knead 4 to 6 minutes on medium-low (KitchenAid speed 2–4) until the dough is smooth and starting to get stretchy. Then add the butter a piece at a time over 2 to 3 minutes, letting each piece mostly incorporate before the next. Continue kneading until the dough is very stretchy (windowpane), about 8 to 10 more minutes. Aim for a final dough temperature of about 78°F — if it's running hot, pause the mixer or finish by hand.

Let rise for about 1 hour in an oiled bowl until doubled in size. This should take about an hour at 75°F.

Shape (11:45 AM)

Divide the dough into six equal pieces (~260 g each) and pre-round into balls. Working one at a time, roll each ball into a flat oval with a rolling pin (this also degasses it), fold it in thirds like a letter, then roll it up into a tight coil.

Place all six coils in a row in one oiled 13x4x4 Pullman pan, seam side down, with the spirals facing the long sides of the pan. Cover with plastic wrap and allow to rise about 40 minutes, until the dough is within about 1/2 inch of the rim — then slide the lid on. (Don't wait longer: if the dough is at the rim before the lid goes on, it will bake dense; if it's much below, the corners won't fill and you'll get rounded edges instead of a square profile.)

Alternative (two 9x5 open-top loaves): place three coils in each of 2 oiled 9 inch by 5 inch bread pans and let rise until about 3/4 of the height of the pan.

The dough is sticky by design — use a lightly oiled surface and hands rather than flour when shaping.

Bake (12:25 PM)

Preheat the oven to 350°F while the dough proofs (start it about 30 minutes before baking).

Pullman (lidded): bake with the lid on at 350°F for 35 to 40 minutes, until the internal temperature reaches 190 to 200°F in the center (slide the lid back to check). No egg wash needed.

Open-top 9x5 loaves: brush with the egg wash and bake at 350°F for 30 to 35 minutes, to the same 190 to 200°F internal temperature. The sugar makes the crust brown fast — tent with foil if it's getting dark before the inside is done.

Remove the loaf from the pan promptly and cool on a wire rack. Let it cool 2 to 3 hours before slicing — the crumb is the whole point of this bread, and cutting warm compresses it into gumminess.
