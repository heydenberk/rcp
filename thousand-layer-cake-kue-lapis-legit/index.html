Thousand Layer Cake Kue Lapis Legit

Dessert

Thousand-layer cake (kue lapis legit), from Coconut & Sambal by Lara Lee. Serves 24. A spiced, buttery Indonesian sponge built from thin golden layers broiled one at a time — cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger and cardamom, 22 egg yolks, and enough butter that it keeps at least a week in the fridge. Also known as spekkoek ('bacon cake') in Dutch, for its streaky appearance. Written for a 9-inch tin; see the note below for the original 8-inch version.

Ingredients

  • 370 grams unsalted butter, softened, plus extra for greasing
  • 150 grams condensed milk
  • 22 units medium egg yolks, at room temperature
  • 150 grams icing (powdered) sugar
  • 70 grams plain (all-purpose) flour
  • 25 grams milk powder
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 tsp ground ginger
  • 1/4 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1/4 tsp sea salt

You will need: a 9-inch round cake tin and an oven with a broiler.

Notes for even layers

  • Use only the broiler, never convection.
  • Measure each layer for consistency: 95 g per layer in a 9-inch tin, spread about 1/8 inch thick. That's roughly 12 layers from this batter. Weigh it — 95 g is about 6 1/2 tablespoons, an awkward number to spoon.
  • Rotate the tin 180° every layer or two. A 9-inch tin covers more of the element than the 8-inch the recipe was written for, so the gap between how fast the center and the edges brown is wider.
  • A layer is done when it turns from pale to golden — watch it constantly, it burns easily.
  • Each layer should take 1 1/2 to 4 minutes, depending on the oven.
  • As the layers get higher you may need to shorten the broiling time or lower the rack.
  • Holes form in the layers if the heat is too high; adjust the temperature as needed.

Lining the tin

Stand the tin on baking parchment, trace the base, and cut just inside the pencil line — the trace sits outside the tin wall, and cutting on the line leaves the round about 1/16 inch too wide, which is exactly enough to make it curl up at the edge and distort the first few layers. Loose-bottomed tin: trace the removable disc instead.

Cut a collar a little longer than the tin's circumference: a 9-inch tin is ~28 inches around, so ~31 inches of strip leaves a few inches of overlap. Trim its height flush with the rim or just under — parchment standing proud of the tin chars under the element.

Butter the tin, press the collar to the sides so the grease holds it, butter lightly over the collar, then lay the round in on top of the collar's bottom edge. Paper won't stay put in a dry tin.

Directions

  1. 10 minutesPreheat the oven to 400°F (350°F convection). Grease and line a 9-inch round cake tin (see above).
  2. 5 minutesUsing an electric whisk, beat the 370 g softened butter with the 150 g condensed milk until pale and fluffy, about 5 minutes.
  3. 8 minutesIn a separate bowl with clean beaters, whisk the 22 egg yolks and 150 g icing sugar together until fluffy and doubled in volume.
  4. 7 minutesOn low speed, add the egg yolk mixture to the whipped butter about 2 tablespoons at a time, beating between additions and being careful not to overmix.
  5. 5 minutesSift the 70 g flour, 25 g milk powder, 1 1/2 tsp cinnamon, 1/4 tsp nutmeg, 1/4 tsp ginger, 1/4 tsp cardamom and 1/4 tsp sea salt into the bowl. Fold gently with a spatula until fully incorporated into a smooth, streak-free batter.
  6. 5 minutesSwitch the oven to broil, with convection off. Spread 95 g of batter over the base of the tin, about 1/8 inch thick. Bang the tin on the work surface to knock out air bubbles. Broil on the top rack until golden, 1 1/2 to 4 minutes.
  7. 5 minutesRemove from the broiler, pop any air bubbles with a cocktail stick, and flatten the layer with an offset spatula. Spread another 95 g of batter on top, bang the tin again, rotate it 180°, and broil another 1 1/2 to 4 minutes until golden.
  8. 45 minutesRepeat step 7 until all the batter is used — about 12 layers in total — keeping the layers even.
  9. 90 minutesCool in the tin for 30 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack to cool completely. Serve in small slices — it is very rich.

In an 8-inch tin, as originally written: 75 g (5 tablespoons) per layer for roughly 15 layers, about an hour of layering, and a cake about a fifth taller. The batter is identical either way — the 9-inch tin spreads the same volume over 27% more floor, so you get fewer, and a shorter cake, not a worse one.

Full example schedule

  1. 1:00 PM: Preheat, grease and line the tin
  2. 1:10 PM: Beat butter and condensed milk
  3. 1:15 PM: Whisk egg yolks and icing sugar
  4. 1:25 PM: Combine, then fold in the dry ingredients
  5. 1:35 PM: Switch to broil; first layer
  6. 1:40 PM–2:30 PM: Remaining ~11 layers, ~4 minutes each, rotating the tin
  7. 2:30 PM: Cool 30 minutes in the tin, then on a rack
  8. 4:00 PM: Slice and serve
