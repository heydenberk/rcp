Scallop Raviolo with Champagne

Serves: 8Estimated Total Time: ~1 hour 15 minutesMakes: ~475g (about 1 pound); 4-6 servingsRest Time: 30 minutes

Scallop Mousse Filling

Ingredient Amount
Sea scallops, cleaned 6 ounces
Egg white 1 large
Kosher salt 1½ teaspoons
Lemon zest, finely grated from ½ large lemon
Heavy cream ⅔ cup

Ravioli

Ingredient Amount
Egg Yolk Dough (see below) 6 ounces, rolled into 2 sheets ~1/32 inch (0.8mm) thick
All-purpose flour for dusting

Sauce

Ingredient Amount
Kosher salt for pasta water
Unsalted butter, cut into pieces 8 tablespoons (4 ounces)
Champagne, Franciacorta, or Prosecco ⅓ cup
Fresh chives, chopped ⅓ cup

Method

Make the Scallop Mousse Filling

In a blender, puree the scallops, egg white, salt, lemon zest, and cream until velvety smooth, about 2 minutes, scraping down the sides as needed. Spoon into a piping bag or gallon-size zipper-lock bag, seal, and use within 1 hour. (Or refrigerate for up to 8 hours.)

Fill the Ravioli

  1. Keep a spray bottle of water nearby
  2. Lay both pasta sheets on a lightly floured work surface; mist lightly as you work to prevent drying
  3. Cut off a corner of the piping bag to make a 1-inch diameter hole
  4. On one sheet, pipe 8 rounds of scallop mousse (~3 tablespoons each), about 3 inches in diameter with 1½-2 inches between rounds
  5. As you pipe, rotate the bag in the center of each round to create a slight depression about 1 inch wide
  6. Lightly mist the dough with water for a good seal
  7. Lay the second sheet evenly over the first
  8. Press the blunt side of a 3-inch ring mold (or drinking glass) over each round to seal
  9. Gently press your thumb into the center of each so the filling looks more like a donut
  10. Press the flat sides of your fingers around the filling on all surfaces to seal
  11. Using a fluted ravioli cutter (or knife), cut evenly between fillings and trim edges to make 4-inch square ravioli

Yield: 8 large ravioli

Storage: Dust with flour and use within 1 hour, or refrigerate in a single layer up to 4 hours, or freeze in a single layer then transfer to zipper-lock bag for up to 3 days. Cook frozen ravioli directly from freezer, adding ~30 seconds to cooking time.

Cook the Ravioli

Bring a large pot of water to a boil and add salt until it tastes like well-seasoned broth. Drop in all the ravioli, stir gently, and cover to quickly return to a gentle boil. Uncover or partially cover and boil, stirring occasionally, until tender but still a little chewy, 2-3 minutes.

Right after dropping the pasta, melt the butter in a deep 12-inch sauté pan over high heat. Add about 1 cup hot pasta water and boil until it thickens slightly into a thin velvety sauce, 2-3 minutes. Add the Champagne and chives and simmer for 1 minute.

Using a spider strainer or large slotted spoon, transfer the cooked ravioli to the pan. Shake and swirl until the sauce becomes creamy and hugs the pasta, about 1 minute (add more pasta water if needed). Season with salt to taste. Toss gently until pasta and sauce marry, leaving just a few spoonfuls of sauce.

Serve

Using a spider strainer or large spoon, place one raviolo on each warmed plate. Spoon remaining sauce over each.

Egg Yolk Dough

A variation of Half-and-Half Egg Dough

Makes: ~475g (about 1 pound); 4-6 servings Rest Time: 30 minutes

Ingredients

Ingredient Amount
Tipo "00" flour (or King Arthur all-purpose) 187.5g (1⅓ cups)
Semolina flour (such as Caputo) 62.5g (½ cup)
Egg yolks 200g (10 large)
Water 2 tablespoons
Extra-virgin olive oil 7g (1½ teaspoons)

Method

By Hand

Combine the flours on your countertop. Build into a mountain and make a well in the center. Pour the egg yolks, water, and oil into the well and mix together with your hands or a fork. Start mixing flour from the edges into the liquid until all or almost all flour is combined. Use a pastry card or bench scraper to gather and chop the flour and liquid together into a paste, then use your hands to press all bits into a cohesive dough, 2-3 minutes.

If dough is crumbly, add water a tablespoon at a time. If wet, add more flour a tablespoon at a time. The consistency should be somewhat soft, like Play-Doh.

Knead until silky, smooth, and firm, about 5 minutes, adding flour if needed to prevent sticking. The dough is ready when it feels somewhat stiff and somewhat soft, just a bit stiffer than Play-Doh. When you tug on it with your fingertips, it should gently pull back into place.

By Stand Mixer

Pour the egg yolks, water, and oil into the bowl of a stand mixer. Mix with the paddle attachment on low speed until blended. Gradually add both flours, mixing just until the dough comes together, 2-3 minutes. Add more flour or water if necessary. It will be raggy at first, then reach a consistency similar to soft Play-Doh.

To knead, switch to medium-low speed until the dough becomes stiffer and you hear the mixer working harder, 2-3 minutes. (For larger quantities, switch to the dough hook.)

Use a dough scraper to scrape dough from bowl and paddle, turn out onto a lightly floured surface. Gather and knead into a ball.

Rest

Shape into a smooth disk, cover, and set aside for at least 30 minutes so the gluten can relax and the water can fully hydrate the flour. The dough should have an even yellow color throughout.

Storage: Wrap in plastic and refrigerate up to 3 days, or vacuum-seal for up to 1 week. Can freeze up to 1 month—thaw overnight in refrigerator before using.

Roll Out the Dough

  1. Cut rested dough into 4 equal pieces (or fewer for larger sheets)
  2. If chilled, let pieces sit covered at room temperature for 10 minutes
  3. Gently flatten or preroll each piece with a rolling pin to fit your pasta roller
  4. Set roller to widest setting, lightly flour dough, and pass through
  5. Dust with flour, brushing off excess
  6. Continue through progressively narrower settings
  7. After going down two settings, fold dough in half crosswise and cut ¼ inch off corners at the fold (this laminates and strengthens the dough)
  8. For ravioli, roll as thin as possible—about 1/32 inch (0.8mm)

Notes

  • Dry scallops: Look for "dry scallops" at your market. Avoid cheaper "wet scallops" treated with sodium tripolyphosphate (STPP), which plumps them with water weight and makes them taste soapy.
  • Wine pairing: The author recommends Franciacorta, the "champagne" of Brescia in northern Italy.
  • Occasion: Save this one for a special occasion like New Year's Eve!
